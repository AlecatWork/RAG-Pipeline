A Guide to Anime and Manga Genres

Many anime genres are defined by the target audience rather than by the story content. The four main demographic categories are shonen, shojo, seinen, and josei.

Shonen is aimed at young teenage boys and typically features action, adventure, friendship, and a hero who grows stronger through effort. Common examples of the genre include Naruto, One Piece, and Dragon Ball.

Shojo is aimed at young teenage girls and tends to focus on romance, relationships, and emotional growth, though many shojo series also include fantasy or adventure. Fruits Basket and Sailor Moon are well-known examples.

Seinen is aimed at adult men and usually features more complex themes, darker tone, and psychological depth. Berserk and Vinland Saga are often cited as seinen titles.

Josei is aimed at adult women and often portrays realistic romance and everyday adult life with a more mature approach than shojo. Nana is a popular example of josei.

Isekai is a genre in which a character from the real world is transported to another world, often a fantasy realm with game-like rules. Mecha features giant piloted robots, and Gundam is the most famous franchise in the genre. Slice of life depicts ordinary daily events, usually with a calm, relaxing mood.
