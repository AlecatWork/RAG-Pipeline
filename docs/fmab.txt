Fullmetal Alchemist: Brotherhood Overview

Fullmetal Alchemist: Brotherhood is a 2009 anime adaptation of the manga written and illustrated by Hiromu Arakawa. It was produced by Studio Bones and runs for 64 episodes. Unlike the 2003 anime adaptation, which diverged from the manga, Brotherhood follows the original manga story from beginning to end.

The story follows brothers Edward and Alphonse Elric, who attempt to bring their dead mother back to life using alchemy. The attempt fails, costing Edward his left leg and Alphonse his entire body. Edward sacrifices his right arm to bind Alphonse's soul to a suit of armor, and he later receives automail prosthetics in place of his lost limbs.

The central rule of alchemy in this world is Equivalent Exchange: to gain something, something of equal value must be lost. This principle shapes the series' themes of sacrifice, responsibility, and the consequences of playing with life and death.

The brothers set out to find the Philosopher's Stone, a legendary object said to let alchemists bypass Equivalent Exchange. Along the way they uncover a conspiracy within the military government of the country of Amestris, involving the homunculi, artificial beings named after the seven deadly sins.

Edward Elric is nicknamed the Fullmetal Alchemist and works as a State Alchemist for the military, which gives him access to research resources. He is sensitive about his short height, and this is a recurring source of humor in the series.

Brotherhood is frequently ranked among the best anime ever made and is known for its tight pacing, a strong supporting cast, and a satisfying ending.
