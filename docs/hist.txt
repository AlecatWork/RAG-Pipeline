A Brief History of Anime

Japanese animation has existed since the early twentieth century, but modern anime is generally traced to Osamu Tezuka, often called the God of Manga. His series Astro Boy premiered on Japanese television in 1963 and was the first popular anime television series.

To produce episodes quickly on a small budget, Tezuka's studio used limited animation, a technique that reuses drawings and relies on still frames, camera movement, and dramatic poses. This approach became a defining feature of the anime style and made weekly television animation possible.

In 1988, the film Akira by Katsuhiro Otomo demonstrated that anime could achieve cinematic quality and appeal to adult audiences. It is widely credited with helping introduce anime to viewers in the West.

Neon Genesis Evangelion, which aired in 1995 and was directed by Hideaki Anno, reshaped the mecha genre with its psychological themes. Cowboy Bebop followed in 1998, a space western known for its jazz soundtrack and its stylish blend of genres.

In the 2000s and 2010s, the growth of internet streaming services such as Crunchyroll made it possible for fans around the world to watch new episodes shortly after they aired in Japan. Anime moved from a niche interest to a global industry.

Today, studios like MAPPA, Ufotable, and Wit Studio mix traditional hand-drawn artwork with computer-generated effects, producing some of the most visually ambitious series ever made.
