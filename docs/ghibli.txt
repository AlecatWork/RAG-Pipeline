Studio Ghibli Overview

Studio Ghibli is a Japanese animation studio founded in 1985 by directors Hayao Miyazaki and Isao Takahata, together with producer Toshio Suzuki. The studio is based in Koganei, Tokyo, and is known for hand-drawn animation, detailed natural environments, and stories that blend everyday life with fantasy.

The studio's name comes from an Italian word for a hot desert wind. Miyazaki chose it to suggest that the studio would "blow a new wind" through the Japanese animation industry.

Spirited Away, released in 2001, follows a ten-year-old girl named Chihiro who becomes trapped in a spirit world and must work in a bathhouse to free her parents. It won the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature in 2003 and became one of the highest-grossing films in Japanese history.

My Neighbor Totoro, released in 1988, tells the story of two sisters who move to the countryside and befriend gentle forest spirits. Totoro, the large furry creature from the film, became the unofficial mascot of Studio Ghibli and appears in the studio's logo.

Princess Mononoke, released in 1997, is an epic set in medieval Japan about the conflict between forest gods and a human iron-working settlement. It deals with themes of environmentalism and the cost of progress, and it is darker and more violent than most of the studio's other films.

Studio Ghibli films are traditionally drawn by hand on paper, which gives them a distinctive painterly look. The studio kept this approach long after most competitors switched to digital production.
